   100→   100→School billing categories such as **Pre-school, Lower Primary, Upper Primary and JHS** must be configurable groupings. A category is not a class, a branch, or a curriculum stage. Schools can include crèche/nursery/KG differently in their own billing categories without corrupting the academic model.
   101→   101→
   102→   102→Support Ghana national curriculum, school enrichment and licensed alternative/international curriculum profiles where a private school needs them. Keep their provenance and grading separate. Never manufacture an accredited curriculum or imply that private schools are exempt from applicable national requirements.
   103→   103→
   104→   104→### Important research conclusions to preserve
   105→   105→
   106→   106→- NaCCA's formal basic-school structure covers KG 1–2, B1–6 and B7–9. Crèche/nursery need a separate early-care model. The revised ECCD policy addresses a wider childhood-development span; that does not make every covered age a formal school grade.
   107→   107→- Government-authored ECCD standards also provide developmental guidance for ages 0–3. Use these to support caregivers and observations, not to refuse admission, rank babies or create reward/sanction systems from milestone scores.
   108→   108→- KG teaching is integrated. NaCCA's KG teacher resource pack describes observation-based assessment and says not to use end-of-month/end-of-term paper-and-pencil tests. Default KG to observations, checklists, portfolios and developmental reports, rather than routing it through the primary/JHS timed examination workflow. Verify later KG assessment tools when building the pack.
   109→   109→- JHS CCP learning areas and BECE examination subjects are related but not identical sets. For example, Physical Education and Health appears in the CCP; do not automatically make every curriculum learning area a BECE paper. Verify which subjects and language options apply to each school/candidate/year.
   110→   110→- Primary and CCP source documents also use different proficiency bands. Store the exact applicable scale and its evidence rather than applying a universal five-band scheme to KG–B9.
   111→   111→- Current WAEC material describes school-candidate BECE continuous assessment at **30%** and external examination at **70%**. This is not a universal weighting for every school's internal term report. Preserve raw scores, maxima, normalized components, the applicable rule and official results separately.
   112→   112→- There is no basis in this brief for calling one configurable KG–JHS GPA scale a compulsory national standard. Support school-defined GPA/averages where wanted, applicable NaCCA proficiency descriptors, raw marks and WAEC result fields as distinct reporting modes.
   113→   113→- Some official legacy pages retain old subject names or dates. OWOP withdrawal information also differs between an older curriculum page and later public statements. Keep historical offerings and resolve current activation using dated authoritative guidance. Do not silently delete a subject or rewrite a past report.
   114→   114→- Externally supplied assessments have NaCCA provider/instrument approval requirements. Support provider certification, instrument-specific approval, versions and verification evidence. Do not market AI-generated questions as NaCCA-approved; confirm the rules applying to any shared/commercial AI assessment service.
   115→   115→
   116→   116→Create a policy conflict register. Ship verified rule packs and transparent school-configurable policies; do not label an unresolved assumption as an official national rule. Obtain a Ghana educator/assessment review of the packs as part of the final acceptance package.
   117→   117→
   118→   118→## 4. Tenancy, school groups and database ownership
   119→   119→
   120→   120→Build **SaaS-capable software**. SaaS describes the delivery/business model; it does not require unrelated schools to share one database. Commercial plans can be finalized later. Keep student-fee collection, platform subscription billing and a school's own accounting separate.
   121→   121→
   122→   122→Our preferred default for new customers in this brief is:
   123→   123→
   124→   124→> **One dedicated operational PostgreSQL database per independent school customer or school-group tenant. Branches belonging to that same customer share its operational database. Unrelated school customers have separate databases.**
   125→   125→
   126→   126→Implement this arrangement from the first schema/provisioning design in the new project. Do not create one database per class, category or physical branch. Design controlled customer mobility and future deployment tiers without introducing unnecessary shared-pool complexity into the initial dedicated-database implementation.
   127→   127→
   128→   128→Retain the meaningful hierarchy:
   129→   129→
   130→   130→**Tenant/customer → organization/legal entity → school/academic institution → campus/physical branch.**
   131→   131→
   132→   132→- A normal independent school has one tenant, one organization, one school and one or more campuses.
   133→   133→- A school group can contain several legal organizations and academic schools within one customer database. Each school can keep its own calendar, grading, fee schedules and principal.
   134→   134→- A physical annex/branch of one school is normally a campus; a separately governed academic institution can be a school under the group. Make this distinction explicit during onboarding.
   135→   135→- Sharing a database does not grant every branch access to every other branch's records. Enforce organization, school, campus, class, assignment and relationship scopes.
   136→   136→- A distinct legal entity retains its own accounting books, payroll obligations and tax identity. Group consolidation must not collapse those ledgers.
   137→   137→
   138→   138→| Example | Operational database arrangement |
   139→   139→|---|---|
   140→   140→| Independent School A, one site | Dedicated customer database A. |
   141→   141→| School A, main campus and two branches | All three sites use customer database A, with scoped campus records and permitted consolidated reporting. |
   142→   142→| Unrelated School B | Dedicated customer database B. |
   143→   143→| Customer group with several schools | One dedicated tenant database by default; separate organizations/schools/campuses and accounting scopes inside it. |
   144→   144→| Future alternative deployment tier | Add an optional shared pool or dedicated cluster only through a documented, tested design that preserves isolation and stable customer IDs; dedicated databases remain the default. |
   145→   145→
   146→   146→### Control plane and tenant plane
   147→   147→
   148→   148→Use a separate platform/control database for customer registration, authoritative database assignments, identity/membership routing, provisioning, entitlements and operational metadata. Keep pupils' school records, marks, detailed finance, health notes and other operational PII in the tenant plane. Define the minimal identity metadata actually needed centrally; do not make unverifiable claims that authentication records can never contain personal information.
   149→   149→
   150→   150→Create one authoritative tenant-to-database registry, for example **`platform.tenant_database_assignments`**, in the new control plane. Enforce one active primary assignment, assignment history, schema version and controlled relocation. Derive tenant authority from authenticated membership and trusted routing, not an arbitrary client `tenant_id` or host header.
   151→   151→
   152→   152→Implement transaction-local PostgreSQL RLS and least-privilege database roles. API users must not inherit migration/BYPASSRLS privileges or own tenant tables. Validate record relationships at database and application boundaries. Cross-database references require explicit application contracts; do not invent PostgreSQL foreign keys across databases.
   153→   153→
   154→   154→Tenant context must cover APIs, jobs, exports, imports, caches, file storage, search, AI retrieval, notifications, reports and synchronization. Use bounded connection pools, safe context cleanup and connection-reuse tests. Retain equivalent isolation safeguards in dedicated databases as defence in depth.
   155→   155→
   156→   156→Provisioning must create the database, migrate schema, seed verified references, create scoped roles, configure backups/storage, link the registry and run readiness checks. Build fleet migration orchestration with canary tenants, retries, failure isolation, schema-version tracking and rollback/runbooks.
   157→   157→
   158→   158→Deliver tested customer export, restore, offboarding and controlled database relocation. Client/device replicas and campus edge databases are replicas/operational nodes of the same customer, not new commercial tenants.

   160→   160→## 5. Technical implementation principles
   161→   161→
   162→   162→Select a modern stack for this fresh project. The following is a practical starting recommendation; improve or replace components when research demonstrates a better fit, documenting the decision and delivering equivalent capabilities:
   163→   163→
   164→   164→- Next.js/React/TypeScript for the responsive web application, school websites and installable PWA.
   165→   165→- NestJS/TypeScript modular backend with explicit domain boundaries and a versioned API.
   166→   166→- PostgreSQL for authoritative operational storage, with a well-designed typed data-access/repository layer such as Kysely/pg or an appropriate alternative.
   167→   167→- Redis and durable workers for queues, scheduled work and transient coordination, never as the only accounting record.
   168→   168→- Flutter with durable local storage for Android, iOS, Windows and macOS applications, or another justified cross-platform approach that demonstrably meets the same offline/native requirements.
   169→   169→- An isolated Python service or worker for ML/optimization when useful. Do not force ordinary CRUD into a second backend merely to claim multiple languages.
   170→   170→- Object storage for documents/media, tenant-scoped retrieval/search and an appropriate observability stack.
   171→   171→
   172→   172→Choose current supported releases using official documentation, validate compatibility and pin reproducible dependencies in the new project's lockfiles. Do not blindly install a version mentioned in an old prompt.
   173→   173→
   174→   174→Use a modular monolith initially where practical. Isolate AI, integrations, synchronization and background work behind clear contracts; split services only when justified. Shared API/domain rules must not be reimplemented inconsistently in five clients.
   175→   175→
   176→   176→Use integer minor units or exact decimals for money, explicit currency, deliberate rounding, server transactions and database constraints. Display Ghana cedi as **₵**, with clear spacing, thousands separators and two decimal places. ISO currency codes may be stored internally or used in provider APIs. Store timestamps with zone-aware semantics and business dates separately. Default to **Africa/Accra**, Ghana phone normalization and an academic-year display such as **2026–2027**.
   177→   177→
   178→   178→Every critical command needs authorization, validation, idempotency, an audit trail, transactional domain updates and reliable event delivery. Use a transactional outbox and idempotent consumers where workflows cross modules. No financial event should disappear because a notification job fails.
   179→   179→
   180→   180→Create ERDs, state diagrams for important workflows, OpenAPI contracts, generated/client-safe types, migration scripts and repository-level quality checks. Do not turn the system into a generic entity editor that cannot express real school rules.
   181→   181→
   182→   182→## 6. Offline-first operation is a core product capability
   183→   183→
   184→   184→An offline badge, cached dashboard, localStorage cache or manual export/import is insufficient. Implement a durable **local-first data and command system**, with automatic recovery and a visible manual Sync action.
   185→   185→
   186→   186→### Three operating conditions
   187→   187→
   188→   188→1. **Connected:** authorized local operations remain responsive; durable changes synchronize with the authoritative services.
   189→   189→2. **Internet outage with school LAN available:** staff can use an installed school/campus edge service for shared attendance, admissions, daily collections, POS, records and approved local exams. Demonstrate this deployment, including local discovery/pairing, authenticated connectivity and safe handover back to cloud connectivity.
   190→   190→3. **A fully disconnected device:** a previously authorized teacher, cashier, conductor or other user continues the permitted downloaded workflows using its own durable local store, then syncs later.
   191→   191→
   192→   192→Use these scenarios to choose a concrete replication model. Define ownership/writer authority for every important aggregate. Do not create uncontrolled multi-master PostgreSQL replication or two competing ledgers. Edge authority, device command capture and cloud finalization must have an explicit contract, including how authority transfers after a failure.
   193→   193→
   194→   194→Native clients should use a transactional local database such as SQLite/Drift with appropriate encryption and OS-keystore protection. The PWA needs IndexedDB or another supported durable browser database, a versioned service worker, controlled asset/data caching and quota/eviction handling. Do not claim browser storage has the same guarantees as an encrypted native database. Check current browser support and provide capability diagnostics and recovery guidance.
   195→   195→
   196→   196→### Offline workflow matrix
   197→   197→
   198→   198→Document and implement read, create, edit, approve, print and sync capabilities per role/module/platform. At minimum:
   199→   199→
   200→   200→| Workflow | Required disconnected behaviour |
   201→   201→|---|---|
   202→   202→| Attendance | Download assigned rosters; capture/revise authorized marks; retain who/when/where; sync with conflict review. |
   203→   203→| Lessons and learning resources | Prepare notes, record delivery/reflection and use downloaded resources. |
   204→   204→| Assessment | Capture assigned raw marks and observations; preserve versions; upload evidence later. |
   205→   205→| Approved school/local exams | Download authorized packages and schedules; save/resume attempts durably; synchronize answers and submission evidence. |
   206→   206→| Admissions/student changes | Capture permitted records and uploads with globally unique IDs; resolve duplicates/references during sync. |
   207→   207→| Cash/daily fee/POS receipts | Record the actual local cash transaction, issue a unique auditable offline receipt, show pending reconciliation, and post it once after validation. |
   208→   208→| Inventory | Capture authorized receipts/issues/counts under an explicit stock-conflict policy. |
   209→   209→| Transport | Use the assigned manifest, record boarding/alighting/handovers/incidents and capture GPS where available. |
   210→   210→| HR/payroll/accounting | Prepare authorized inputs, drafts and local work; execute only offline-authorized postings/approvals, with clear status and later reconciliation. |
   211→   211→| Messages | Draft/queue messages; send and update delivery status after the relevant channel becomes reachable. |
   212→   212→
   213→   213→Remote bank/MoMo settlement, cloud AI calls, internet messages and another device's unsynchronized information cannot become live facts without connectivity. Show **recorded locally**, **pending provider verification**, **synchronized** or **conflict** accurately. A parent must not see stale GPS presented as a live bus location. A cached balance must identify its last synchronization time and pending local changes.
   214→   214→
   215→   215→### Synchronization protocol
   216→   216→
   217→   217→- Globally unique IDs, operation IDs, device IDs, authenticated membership context and explicit schema/protocol versions.
   218→   218→- Atomic local transaction for a business change plus its outbox command. App termination or power loss must not acknowledge an undurable operation.
   219→   219→- At-least-once transport with idempotent application and deduplication. Never promise magical exactly-once network delivery; demonstrate one business effect for repeated delivery.
   220→   220→- Entity/base versions, dependencies and domain validation; server-derived ownership and permission checks on every replay.
   221→   221→- Transactional server change feed, tenant/device/scope-bound opaque cursors, ordered paging, acknowledgement, resumable pull/push and attachment queues.
   222→   222→- Design and document a correct cursor protocol in this new project. Sequence allocation alone is not a safe commit-order cursor: test transactions committing out of order so a client cannot advance past an unseen committed change. Define a correct publication/high-watermark scheme.
   223→   223→- Backoff, jitter, bounded batches, compression, partial failure isolation, dead-letter handling, poison-operation diagnostics and safe retry.
   224→   224→- Durable tombstones with a documented retention/rebootstrap policy, including devices offline beyond retention.
   225→   225→- Large file checksums, resumable uploads, file permissions and no premature deletion of evidence.
   226→   226→- Persist pulled changes and the corresponding cursor atomically. Never advance the cursor before durable application.
   227→   227→- Apply permission-scope removals and changes as well as additions. A teacher reassigned away from a class must not retain its current records indefinitely.
   228→   228→- Separate local storage, queues, search and caches by authenticated tenant, user/membership and applicable scope. School switching/logout must not show a previous customer's data.
   229→   229→- Offline leases for authorized users/devices, local lock/re-authentication, clock-tamper handling, device revocation, secure key handling and bounded offline retention. Revocation takes effect remotely when the device reconnects; do not claim guaranteed immediate remote wiping of a disconnected device.
   230→   230→- Reject unauthorized posting without silently discarding captured evidence of actual cash received or child handovers. Define restricted evidence recovery/review and explicit resolution before local cleanup can destroy unresolved evidence. Recovering evidence must not give a revoked user new posting or viewing rights.
   231→   231→- Consistent reconnection behaviour on foreground, app launch, network recovery and manual sync. Browser/iOS background execution is constrained; core correctness must not depend on a background sync API always running.
   232→   232→- Safe app/schema upgrades while unsent commands exist, mixed client versions, tenant relocation and edge/cloud failover.
   233→   233→
   234→   234→### Conflict rules are domain-specific
   235→   235→
   236→   236→Never use universal last-write-wins for money, marks, child handovers, stock or approvals.
   237→   237→
   238→   238→- Posted money entries are immutable. Corrections are linked reversals/credit notes/reallocations with approvals; duplicates return the original result.
   239→   239→- Concurrent spending of the same advance balance needs reservation, bounded offline authority or a visible reconciliation queue. Do not create globally available funds twice.
   240→   240→- Attendance has a natural uniqueness boundary and correction history; resolve competing authorized edits visibly.
   241→   241→- Marks have ownership, moderation and publication locks; a late offline mark must not silently overwrite a published result.
   242→   242→- Stock must expose oversubscription/negative-stock exceptions under policy; never invent missing units.
   243→   243→- Student identity conflicts and relationship changes require a controlled merge/review process.
   244→   244→- Transport manifests and handover events preserve conflicting evidence and alert authorized staff.
   245→   245→- Conflicts must be understandable to nontechnical staff, with the original values, proposed changes, actor and resolution history.
   246→   246→
   247→   247→Deliver a sync operations console showing pending counts, last success, failures by cause, authorized retry/review actions and edge/node health. Logs must help resolve a school outage without exposing another school's data.
